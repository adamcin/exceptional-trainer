## The Color Matching Mindset

Great color matching requires:
- **Observation**: Train your eye to see color relationships
- **Reference analysis**: Study the target environment's color
- **Subtlety**: Most adjustments should be barely noticeable individually
- **Iteration**: Match → evaluate → refine → repeat
- **Context**: Colors are relative—what matters is harmony, not absolute accuracy

## Key Concepts

### Color Harmony
When composite elements share color characteristics:
- Similar color temperature (warm/cool)
- Similar saturation levels
- Similar contrast ranges
- Similar color casts (if any)

### Color Temperature
- **Warm**: Reds, oranges, yellows (daylight, fire, incandescent light)
- **Cool**: Blues, cyans, greens (shade, overcast, fluorescent light)
- **Neutral**: Balanced, no obvious warm/cool bias

Historical paintings often have warm, golden tones from aging and oil paint oxidation.

### Color Adaptation
Your eye adjusts to overall color cast—what looks "normal" in one image may have a strong color bias. When compositing, this must be matched.
