# Precision Masking Techniques

Welcome to Module 2! Now that you've mastered layer organization, it's time to learn the most critical skill for seamless compositing: precision masking.

## What You'll Learn

This module focuses on creating perfect selections and masks that make composites look photorealistic:

- **Advanced Selection Tools**: Master Quick Selection, Object Selection, and manual selection tools
- **Hair and Fur Selection**: Conquer the hardest masking challenge—fine details like hair
- **Edge Refinement**: Use Select and Mask workspace to perfect selection edges
- **Channel-Based Masking**: Leverage color channels for complex masking workflows
- **Combining Techniques**: Know which tool to use for each situation

## Why This Is Critical

Your satirical "Declaration of Independence" project requires:
- Cleanly separating Elon Musk's head from its background
- Preserving hair detail and edge quality
- Creating masks that blend seamlessly with the 1818 painting style
- Handling varying lighting and background complexity
- Making selections precise enough to fool the eye

Poor masking is the #1 giveaway of amateur compositing. Halos, hard edges, and lost details destroy realism instantly.

## The Masking Mindset

Great masking requires:
- **Patience**: Rushing creates sloppy edges
- **Zoom**: Work at 100% or higher magnification for edges
- **Multiple passes**: First pass rough, second pass refine, third pass perfect
- **Tool combination**: No single tool solves every problem
- **Reference checking**: Toggle mask view to verify quality

## Key Concepts

### Selection vs. Mask
- **Selection** (marching ants): Temporary, defines an area
- **Mask** (grayscale image): Permanent, controls layer visibility
- You create selections, then convert them to masks

### Soft vs. Hard Edges
- **Hard edge**: Sharp transition, good for geometric objects
- **Soft edge**: Gradual transition, good for organic subjects
- **Feathering**: How far the soft transition extends

### Color Contamination
When selecting subjects against colored backgrounds, edge pixels often contain background color. This creates color "fringing" or halos in the composite. Module 3 will address color correction, but good masking prevents most of this.

## Common Masking Challenges

You'll learn to handle:
1. **Hair against busy backgrounds**: The ultimate masking test
2. **Semi-transparent elements**: Veils, smoke, glass
3. **Motion blur**: Moving subjects with blur trails
4. **Complex edges**: Tree branches, fur, fabric texture
5. **Low contrast edges**: Subject and background similar colors
6. **Mixed edge types**: Hard edges (shoulders) + soft edges (hair) on same subject

## What Makes a Good Mask?

Evaluate every mask by:
1. **Edge quality**: No halos, fringing, or choppiness
2. **Detail preservation**: Hair strands, fine textures retained
3. **Transition appropriateness**: Hard edges where needed, soft where needed
4. **Color purity**: No background color contamination
5. **Invisibility**: Mask is undetectable in final composite
