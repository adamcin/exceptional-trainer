# Challenge: Change Time of Day in Photo

## Objective

Transform a daylight photograph to candlelight/golden hour lighting through advanced color grading—simulating your project's core challenge (modern photos to 1818 candlelight painting).

## Scenario

You have modern daylight portraits. The Declaration of Independence painting shows early 19th-century indoor lighting (candles, oil lamps). Transform your portraits to match that warm, golden, soft lighting environment.

## Requirements

**Source Image**: Portrait photographed in daylight (neutral/cool lighting)

**Target**: Warm candlelight/golden hour appearance:
- Warm color temperature (yellow-orange)
- Reduced contrast (soft shadows)
- Golden highlights
- Warm shadows (not black)
- Overall softer, more romantic feel

## Challenge Tasks

## Task 1: Analyze Target Lighting

Study historical paintings lit by candlelight:

1. Sample colors from "Declaration of Independence" or similar
2. Document:
   - Highlight color (usually warm yellow-orange)
   - Shadow color (warm brown, never black)
   - Midtone warmth (golden)
   - Overall color cast (amber/sepia)
   - Contrast level (compressed range)

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Target lighting characteristics documented
- [ ] Color samples collected
- [ ] Clear understanding of goal

## Task 2: Overall Warming

Initial broad adjustment:

1. Curves adjustment:
   - **Blue channel**: Drag down significantly (adds yellow)
   - **Red channel**: Drag up moderately (adds warmth)
   - Apply to entire tonal range
2. Result should be noticeably warmer

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Image significantly warmed
- [ ] No longer looks like daylight
- [ ] Foundation for further refinement laid

## Task 3: Split-Tone for Candlelight

Highlights and shadows need different treatment:

1. Curves - Blue channel:
   - **Highlights** (upper right): Drag DOWN more (very yellow highlights)
   - **Shadows** (lower left): Drag DOWN moderately (warm, not black)
2. Curves - Red channel:
   - **Highlights**: Slightly UP (peachy-golden)
   - **Shadows**: Neutral to slightly up (avoid pure black)

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Highlights are golden-yellow
- [ ] Shadows are warm brown/amber
- [ ] No cool tones remaining
- [ ] Split-tone creates depth

## Task 4: Contrast Reduction

Candlelight is soft:

1. Curves - RGB channel:
   - Lift blacks (bottom-left point up)
   - Slightly reduce whites (top-right point down)
   - Results in flatter, softer look
2. S-curve should be inverse (less contrast, not more)

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Contrast reduced from original
- [ ] Blacks are not pure black
- [ ] Whites are not blown out
- [ ] Softer, more painted look

## Task 5: Color Cast and Mood

Final color treatment:

1. **Photo Filter** adjustment (optional):
   - Choose "Warming Filter (85)" or "Sepia"
   - Density: 25-50%
   - Adds unified warm cast

2. **Color Balance**:
   - Shadows: +20 Yellow, +10 Red
   - Midtones: +30 Yellow, +20 Red
   - Highlights: +35 Yellow, +25 Red

3. **Hue/Saturation**:
   - Slightly reduce overall saturation (-10 to -20%)
   - Aged paintings are less saturated than modern photos

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Unified warm color cast applied
- [ ] Saturation appropriate for historical look
- [ ] Mood matches candlelight/oil lamp era
- [ ] No remnants of daylight appearance

## Task 6: Texture and Grain (Optional but Recommended)

Match painted texture:

1. Add noise: Filter > Noise > Add Noise (2-5%)
2. Add texture overlay (canvas texture if available)
3. Slight Gaussian Blur (0.5-1px) for softness
4. Reduces digital sharpness

**Success Criteria**:
- [ ] Added appropriate texture
- [ ] Reduces "digital photo" look
- [ ] Enhances period feel

## Overall Success Criteria

- [ ] Transformed from daylight to candlelight appearance
- [ ] Warm color throughout (yellow-orange-amber)
- [ ] Reduced contrast (soft lighting)
- [ ] No cool tones remaining
- [ ] Matches historical painting lighting reference
- [ ] Natural appearance (not over-processed)
- [ ] Skin tones still believable
- [ ] Completed in under 40 minutes

## Common Issues

### "Too orange/fake tan"
**Solution**: Reduce red channel adjustment, rely more on yellow (blue channel down).

### "Lost all contrast"
**Solution**: Don't lift blacks too much. Some contrast needed for dimension.

### "Skin looks dead"
**Solution**: Saturation reduced too much, or lost red in skin. Add back selectively with masked adjustment.

### "Still looks like daylight"
**Solution**: Not warm enough. Be more aggressive with blue channel down, red channel up.
