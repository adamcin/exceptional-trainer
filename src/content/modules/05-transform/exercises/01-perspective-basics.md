# Exercise: Understanding Perspective in Composites

## Learning Objective

Understand perspective principles to create geometrically accurate composites.

## Perspective Fundamentals

**Perspective**: How 3D space is represented on 2D surface.

**Key concepts**:
- Objects appear smaller with distance
- Parallel lines converge to vanishing points
- Viewing angle affects object appearance
- Scale relative to position in space

## Part 1: One-Point Perspective

Single vanishing point (typical in corridors, roads, railroad tracks).

**Characteristics**:
- All depth lines converge to one point
- Horizontal/vertical lines remain parallel
- Front faces of objects parallel to picture plane

**In composites**: If background has strong one-point perspective, subjects must follow same vanishing point.

## Part 2: Two-Point Perspective

Two vanishing points (typical in room corners, buildings).

**Characteristics**:
- Two sets of parallel lines, each converging to separate vanishing point
- Vertical lines remain vertical
- Most common in architectural scenes

**In composites**: Subjects must align with both vanishing points if interacting with geometric environment.

## Part 3: Three-Point Perspective

Three vanishing points (looking up/down at tall structures).

**Characteristics**:
- Even verticals converge to third vanishing point
- Extreme viewing angles
- Less common

## Part 4: Scale and Distance

**Distance affects scale**:
- Closer = larger
- Farther = smaller
- Must be proportional

**Measuring technique**:
1. Identify two objects at different distances with known relationship (e.g., people)
2. Measure ratio
3. Apply ratio to new subject

**Example**: If background figure is 6 inches tall and midground figure is 3 inches tall, midground is 2x farther. New subject at midground must be 3 inches.

## Part 5: Viewing Angle

Head angle must match body angle:
- Looking up at figure = see underside of chin/nose
- Looking down = see top of head more
- Eye level = neutral viewing angle

**Declaration of Independence**: Generally eye-level view with slight variations.

## Part 6: Horizon Line

**Horizon line** = viewer's eye level in scene.

Finding horizon line:
1. Identify vanishing points
2. Draw line connecting them
3. That's the horizon

**Subject placement**:
- Subject eyes at horizon = same height as viewer
- Subject eyes above horizon = subject shorter than viewer
- Subject eyes below horizon = subject taller than viewer

## Verification Checklist

- [ ] Understand one/two/three-point perspective
- [ ] Can identify vanishing points in images
- [ ] Can find horizon line
- [ ] Understand scale/distance relationship
- [ ] Understand viewing angle concept
- [ ] Can analyze perspective in reference images
